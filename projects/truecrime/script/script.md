The night shift at the downtown precinct began like any other, until the call came in at 2:17 a.m.

A body was found behind the old textile mill on River Street. No wallet. No phone. Just a single red scarf tied in a bow around the victim's neck.

Detective Mara Voss arrived first. She walked the scene once, slowly, and said nothing. The rain had washed away most of the evidence, but not everything.

Security footage from a bodega two blocks away showed a figure in a dark coat, limping, heading west at exactly 1:44 a.m. The timestamp matched the estimated time of death.

Forensics recovered one partial print. It belonged to a man reported missing three weeks earlier. A man with no known enemies. A man who, according to his coworkers, simply stopped coming to work one Tuesday and was never seen again.

The case went cold. Until last week, when an identical red scarf turned up in a donation bin outside the precinct.

Someone knows what happened that night. And they are watching.
